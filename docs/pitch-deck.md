# Two-slide pitch plan

Draft framing for the [demo video](demo-video.md), supported by the [build plan](build-plan.md). This is a slide-content plan, not a finished deck.

## Role of the slides

Use two slides to frame one continuous demonstration: promise at 0–10 seconds, product at 10–80 seconds, learning at 80–90 seconds. That is **20 seconds of slides and 70 seconds of product**. Open with what someone can do; don't add a conventional problem-first deck or an internship pain-point interview.

Done means:

- The opening gives the viewer a clear reason to watch the product.
- The closing connects an observed comparison to a practical next test.
- Every claim is supported by the actual prototype or clearly labelled as a proposal.

## Slide 1: the promise, 0–10 seconds

Purpose: explain the action and value before showing the interface. Use one headline and one short supporting line. Product name remains undecided.

Draft on-slide copy:

```text
Enter an idea. Assess its commercial potential.

For founders and VCs: explore buyers, competitors and adoption factors, then test an assumption.
```

Draft spoken transition:

```text
Let's enter one idea, choose an audience and set a price. Then we'll inspect the evidence and see what changes when we adjust one assumption.
```

Keep the visual treatment simple enough to read within ten seconds. Move directly into the form. “Quantitative VC” may help explain the concept in discussion, but it is not the product name or a validated investment model.

## Slide 2: the learning, 80–90 seconds

Purpose: show what the interaction helped us learn and identify the next decision. The actual finding cannot be written until the selected example has been researched and run.

Draft on-slide structure, with explicit unfinished fields:

```text
What changed. What to test next.

Observed change: [Replace with the actual comparison finding.]
Next test: [Replace with one real-world test of its weakest assumption.]
```

These brackets are editorial instructions, not submission-ready copy. Replace them only with verified output. A next test could be a buyer interview or a price experiment; choose one that addresses the uncertainty shown in the demo. Don't claim simulated interest validates willingness to pay.

Draft spoken framing:

```text
The value is seeing which assumption changes the assessment, why it changes, and what we should test next.
```

Pair that framing with the concrete finding from the recorded run. Avoid a closing accuracy percentage, invented confidence interval or promise of startup success.

## Submission fit and review

The official video format is two minutes, including a working prototype. Use the [video wrapper](demo-video.md#official-submission-wrapper) to add 30 seconds of context and actual technology information after the core narrative. The supplied rules specify no separate deck or live-pitch duration.

Before recording, check that the slide language matches working features; both founders and VCs are included without implying investment-return predictions; and the ending reflects the actual comparison. The written submission must separately explain the problem, how the solution works and technologies used. A promise-first video does not remove that written requirement.
