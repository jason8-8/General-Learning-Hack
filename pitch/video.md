## Narration

```text
Learn from the future. Shape the present.

Throughout human evolution, we’ve learnt from experience.
From failure.
From what came before us.

But what if we could explore the outcomes before making a decision?

We’re automating market research to help founders answer:
is your product idea worth pursuing?

Let’s test an AI travel assistant
that helps people plan and navigate trips.

Who would pay for it?
And why choose it over ChatGPT or free travel tools?

Enter your idea and confirm the brief.

The system researches the market
and builds a simulated audience grounded in that evidence.

Explore what different customers value,
what they might pay for,
and what could stop them.

Open a response to inspect the profile and reasoning behind it.
Calculations turn the responses into comparisons,
with commercial assumptions shown separately.

Then bring in feedback from real customers.

Compare what the simulation predicted
with what people actually told you.

See where it missed something.
Update the audience with what you’ve learnt.
And run the assessment again.

The original results stay visible,
so you can see what changed and why.

New customer evidence then tests
whether those changes actually improve the predictions.

You leave with an audience to investigate,
a recommendation to test,
and a clearer view of what’s still uncertain.

We’re automating trial and error,
so we can learn from possible futures
and make better choices today.

Learn from the future.
Shape the present.
```

The 1,000-respondent target is deliberately absent from spoken claims until a measured run exists. Show the actual completed count on screen. The quantitative layer is not a calibrated commercial-success probability.

## Recording gates

- Real interface footage only for implemented interactions. Concept scenes explicitly labelled.
- Use actual completed/failed counts. Do not claim 1,000 until it runs; no decorative swarm implying a larger population.
- Identify the engine that ran. MiroFish remains unverified. Dated cached runs stay labelled; no fabricated live progress.
- Real interviews have not been provided here. Never describe authored example feedback as a real interview.
- Changed output is not proof of improvement. Use untouched evidence for evaluation, or say evaluation pending.
- If the feedback loop is not implemented, replace its narration with: “The next step is to bring in real customer feedback, compare it with these predictions, and use the differences to refine the audience.” Show planned functionality, not a fake live interaction.
- No implementation restart or new API spend is authorised.
