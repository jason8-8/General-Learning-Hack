# Travel assistant: commercial demo revision

Checked 20 September 2026. Primary sources:

- Mindtrip: https://mindtrip.ai/ — free AI travel app; itineraries, group planning and booking organisation. Generic itinerary generation is a weak paid differentiator.
- Wanderlog: https://help.wanderlog.com/hc/en-us/articles/13302997563547-Is-Wanderlog-free — free core planning and collaboration. No exact current Pro price asserted because source pages and storefront offers differ.
- TripIt Pro: https://www.tripit.com/web/pro — US$49/year, including alerts and alternative-flight assistance. Annual USD benchmark retained in its original unit; not converted into a GBP per-trip recommendation.

Recommendation (inference, not a market finding): test a trip-readiness pack for 7–14 day multi-city trips. Proposed value is checking transfer/timing conflicts, budget constraints and fallback plans rather than merely generating an itinerary. This is a concept under evaluation, not a built travel feature or proven competitive advantage.

The demo compares basic itinerary generation with this proposed pack across five buying situations. Its 1,000 cases have equal segment allocation for demonstration, not representative market weights. Eligibility fractions, price-response midpoints, concept multipliers and sensitivity ranges are explicitly unvalidated assumptions in the generator and UI. Research does not determine conversion rates, willingness to pay or a revenue forecast.

Readiness assumptions by segment: first-time multi-city (.38, £15 midpoint); complex families (.34, £18); group organisers (.26, £12); simple city breaks (.10, £7); frequent work (.08, £10). Logistic slope £5. Basic itinerary uses .35× eligibility and .6× price thresholds. Low/high scenario counts are .6×/1.4× base, not confidence intervals. At £10, deterministic discretisation yields 159/1000 readiness choices and 31/1000 basic choices. These are illustrative consequences of the assumptions, not researched findings. Sensible-looking numbers are not evidence of validity.

The old generic friction chart and arbitrary majority-paying thresholds are superseded. Do not use their percentages in the new pitch. Next real test: recruit people with upcoming multi-city trips, show identical priced sample deliverables and free alternatives, and record actual choices without conditioning on the simulated answer.
